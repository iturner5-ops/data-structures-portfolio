NFL QB Stats before vs after injury

Problem Definition

Do quarterbacks who undergo shoulder surgery show higher long-term career longevity (measured in active seasons or games played post-injury) compared to those who opt for non-surgical rehab? This is my research question that I will be trying to answer. My target variable is Post-Injury Performance Metrics which is how well the quarterback does after he got injured. This is a regression problem because it is predicting future QB performances following a shoulder injury. The people who might benefit from this model and predictions are NFL coaching staffs, due to needing to know if their QB should get surgery depending on if their QB suffered a shoulder injury. Reporters and analysts that deal with sports and specifically the NFL also will benefit due to having a story to cover on why QB's who get shoulder surgery perform better than QB's who get hurt and opt to not get surgery. NFL medical staff will benefit due to understanding more about injuries and being able to give a QB advice on why they should get surgery if they suffer a shoulder injury. This problem is worth investigating because the model shows why QB's should opt to get surgery on their shoulder if they suffer a shoulder injury because the QB will have better long term success if they get surgery than if they were to opt out of surgery.
